\section{Preliminaries: directed information and testing duality}
\label{app:prelim}

We write $X^T=(X_1,\dots,X_T)$; all logarithms are natural (nats). Standard
facts (chain rule, DPI, concavity of mutual information in the input
distribution, strict concavity of entropy, Pinsker's inequality) are used as
in \citet{cover_thomas2006}.

\begin{definition}[Directed information; Massey 1990, eq.~(7) \citep{massey1990}]
$I(X^T\!\to\!Y^T):=\sum_{t=1}^T I(X^t;Y_t\given Y^{t-1})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Causally conditioned directed information; Kramer 2003,
\S II-C Def.~4, eq.~(4) \citep{kramer2003}]
$I(X^T\!\to\!Y^T\,\|\,Z^T):=\sum_{t=1}^T I(X^t;Y_t\given Y^{t-1},Z^t)$.
This \emph{non-anticipating} conditioning ($Z^t$) is the convention used
throughout the paper: it is what makes the mediation lemma
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mediation}) true, since conditioning on future components of
$Z$ can open collider paths through their descendants
(Appendix~\ref{app:t2}).\footnote{We verified against the published original
that Kramer's Definition~4 is the causal variant with $Z^t$ conditioning; the
full-sequence variant is Marko's convention. A direction misprint in Kramer's
printed Property~2 counterexample was verified and corrected: with $X_1\perp
Y_1$ fair bits, $X_2=X_1$, $Y_2=X_1\oplus Y_1$, one has
$I(Y^2\!\to\!X^2)=0$ while $I(X^2;Y^2)=1$ bit---the printed sentence reverses
the direction of the first DI; our regression tests encode the corrected
version.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[MI = DI under randomized exogenous probes]\label{lem:probe}
Let $I^T$ be exogenous to the agent (experimenter-randomized,
$I^T\ind$ agent history) and non-anticipative (given $I^t$, the choice of
$I_{t+1:T}$ is independent of $A^t$). Then
$I(I^T;A^T)=I(I^T\!\to\!A^T)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each $t$, the mutual-information chain rule gives
\[
I(I^T;A_t\given A^{t-1})=I(I^t;A_t\given A^{t-1})
+I(I_{t+1:T};A_t\given I^t,A^{t-1}).
\]
No claim that ``conditioning reduces mutual information'' is used (and that
claim is false in general). Non-anticipation makes $A_t$'s structural
generation independent of future inputs $I_{t+1:T}$ given $(I^t,A^{t-1})$;
exogenous randomization supplies the no-feedback condition, so the second
term is zero. Hence
$I(I^T;A_t\given A^{t-1})=I(I^t;A_t\given A^{t-1})$ term by term, and summing
over $t$ proves equality with directed information. This is the
randomized-probe instance of Massey's no-feedback identity; the closed agent
loop would otherwise violate its premise.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[From the probe lemma to the certified bound: the bypass
premise]\label{rem:probe-bridge}
Lemma~\ref{lem:probe} identifies the observable $I(I^T;A^T)$ with the
\emph{unconditioned} directed information $I(I^T\!\to\!A^T)$; the estimand of
the certified bound is the \emph{causally conditioned}
$\Fext=I(I^T\!\to\!A^T\|S^T)$. The bridge is the \emph{bypass premise}
$I^t\ind S^t\given A^{t-1}$ (the probe acts outside the belief-mediation
path). Under it, $I(I^t;S^t\given A^{t-1})=0$, so the identity
\[
I(I^t;A_t\given A^{t-1},S^t)-I(I^t;A_t\given A^{t-1})
= I(I^t;S^t\given A^{t-1},A_t)-I(I^t;S^t\given A^{t-1})
\]
has a non-negative right-hand side for every $t$: conditioning on $S^t$
is non-decreasing, and $F_{ext}^{obs}\le\Fext$. Without the premise the
direction can reverse; the mediation witness ($S=I\oplus N$ with
$N\sim\mathrm{Bern}(0.1)$, $A=S$: $\Fext=0$ while $I(I;A)=0.531$~bit) has the
probe entering through $S$, and then the plug-in overestimates the external
flow. The premise is not a regularity condition but the \emph{semantic
certification boundary} of \eqref{eq:aorg-interval}: only bypass-semantics
probes carry the one-sided reading, and adaptive (state-correlated) probes
violate it (BB-2). We register the boundary rather than extend the theorem
over the mediation case, where no black-box direction is available.
\end{remark}

The implementation also checks a two-step binary SCM with independent fair

\begin{lemma}[Testing duality]\label{lem:tv-testing}
For any test $\psi\in[0,1]$ (randomization allowed):
$P_0(\psi=1)+P_\gamma(\psi=0)\ge 1-\TV(P_0,P_\gamma)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$P_0(\psi=1)-P_\gamma(\psi=1)\le\TV$, so
$P_0(\psi=1)+1-P_\gamma(\psi=1)\ge 1-\TV$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Le Cam-type undetectability]
If $\TV(P_\gamma,P_0)\le\varepsilon$, every level-$\alpha$ test has power
$\le\alpha+\varepsilon$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[Perturbation bound for contraction fixed points]
\label{lem:contraction}
Let $T_\theta:\Theta\to\Theta$ be a contraction with modulus $c<1$ in a common
metric $d$, with $d(T_\theta(x),T_{\theta'}(x))\le L\,d_\Theta(\theta,\theta')$.
Then the unique fixed points satisfy
$d(u^*(\theta),u^*(\theta'))\le\frac{L}{1-c}d_\Theta(\theta,\theta')$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$d(u^*,u'^*)\le d(u^*,T_\theta(u'^*))+d(T_\theta(u'^*),T_\theta(u^*))
+d(T_\theta(u^*),u'^*)\le L\,d_\Theta(\theta,\theta')+c\,d(u^*,u'^*)$;
rearrange. This lemma bounds goal drift by belief drift (the $\gamma\to\Agoal$
transmission) via $T=\Endorse_{B,M}$.
\end{proof}

\section{The representation theorem: full proofs}\label{app:representation}

Recall (A0)--(A6) from Definition~\ref{def:a16}. We use only the
scale-invariance proof below. Continuity, strict monotonicity, and
biseparability alone do not imply idempotency and hence do not yield the
quasi-arithmetic-mean representation previously invoked: for example,
$F(x,y)=(x+y)/4$ has $F(x,x)=x/2\neq x$. Removing that route does not affect
the main theorem, which does not use a separability representation.

\begin{lemma}[AX6b excludes $p<0$ within the power-mean family]\label{lem:ax6b}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The geometric mean satisfies (A6) exactly:
$F(\lambda\odot x)/F(\lambda\odot y)=F(x)/F(y)$. For $p<0$, with $n=2$,
$w=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, write $\Psi(x)=\sum_iw_ix_i^p$ and note
$M_p=\Psi^{1/p}$ is \emph{decreasing} in $\Psi$ ($1/p<0$). Take $1/p$-powers
$a:=3.5^{1/p}$, $b:=2^{1/p}$, $\mu:=4^{1/p}$ (all in $(0,1)$), and set
$x=(a,1)$, $y=(b,b)$, $\lambda=(1,\mu)\in(0,1]^2$. Then
$\Psi(x)=(3.5+1)/2=2.25>\Psi(y)=2$, so $M_p(x)<M_p(y)$ ($y\succ x$); but
$\Psi(\lambda\odot x)=(3.5+4)/2=3.75<\Psi(\lambda\odot y)=(2+8)/2=5$, so
$M_p(\lambda\odot x)>M_p(\lambda\odot y)$: the order flips, violating (A6).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Homogeneous extension from the bounded cube]\label{lem:homog-ext}
Let $F:(0,1]^n\to(0,1]$ satisfy \textup{(A1)}, \textup{(A2)},
\textup{(A5)}$_{loc}$ ($F(tx)=tF(x)$ whenever $tx\in(0,1]^n$), and
\textup{(A6)}. Then $\tilde F(x):=F(sx)/s$ for any $s>0$ with
$sx\in(0,1]^n$ is well-defined on $\mathbb{R}_{++}^n$ (independent of $s$),
continuous, strictly increasing in each coordinate, homogeneous of degree
one, order-invariant under coordinatewise rescaling, and agrees with $F$ on
$(0,1]^n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Well-definedness: if $s_1x,s_2x\in(0,1]^n$ with $s_1\le s_2$, then
$(s_1/s_2)\,(s_2x)=s_1x\in(0,1]^n$ and \textup{(A5)}$_{loc}$ gives
$F(s_1x)=(s_1/s_2)F(s_2x)$, i.e.\ $F(s_1x)/s_1=F(s_2x)/s_2$. Agreement on
$(0,1]^n$ is the case $s=1$. Homogeneity: for $t>0$ and small enough $s$,
$\tilde F(tx)=F(stx)/s=t\,\tilde F(x)$. Monotonicity and continuity are
inherited from $F$ on a neighborhood. Scale invariance of the induced order:
$F(x)\ge F(y)\iff F(\lambda\odot x)\ge F(\lambda\odot y)$ for
$\lambda\in(0,1]^n$ extends to $\tilde F$ and arbitrary
$\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^n$ as follows. Choose $c>0$ with
$c\lambda\in(0,1]^n$ (for example, $c=(2\max_i\lambda_i)^{-1}$), and then
choose $s>0$ small enough that $sx,sy,sc\lambda\odot x,sc\lambda\odot y$
all lie in the cube. Homogeneity reduces the desired equivalence to the
comparison of $sx$ with $sy$ under the admissible multiplier $c\lambda$;
(A6) applies on the cube, and homogeneity on both sides removes $s$ and $c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[route B: scale invariance + homogeneity suffice]
\label{thm:routeb}
Let $F:\mathbb{R}_{++}^n\to\mathbb{R}_{++}$ be continuous, strictly increasing
in each coordinate, homogeneous of degree one, with coordinatewise scale
invariance of the induced order (for all $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^n$:
$F(x)\ge F(y)\iff F(\lambda\odot x)\ge F(\lambda\odot y)$), and $F(1,\dots,1)=1$.
Then $F(x)=\prod_ix_i^{w_i}$ with $w\gg0$, $\sum_iw_i=1$. The resulting
product extends continuously to $[0,\infty)^n$, where it satisfies zero
annihilation ($F(x)=0\iff\min_ix_i=0$); applied to a channel aggregator on
$(0,1]^n$, the theorem is invoked on the homogeneous extension of
Lemma~\ref{lem:homog-ext} and the conclusion restricts back to the cube.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(1) In log coordinates, $g(u):=\log F(e^{u_1},\dots,e^{u_n})$; scale
invariance becomes \emph{translation} invariance of the induced order:
$g(u)\ge g(v)\iff g(u+c)\ge g(v+c)$ for all $c\in\mathbb{R}^n$.
(2) \emph{Consistency lemma}: for fixed $d$, the proposition
``$g(x+d)\ge g(x)$'' is independent of $x$ (apply translation invariance with
$c=x'-x$).
(3) Let $D:=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^n: g(x+d)\ge g(x)\ \forall x\}$; well-defined by
(2). Properties: $D$ closed (continuity); $D+D\subseteq D$;
$D\cup(-D)=\mathbb{R}^n$ (trichotomy from the consistency lemma); and
$W:=D\cap(-D)=\{d:g(x+d)=g(x)\ \forall x\}$ is a closed subspace. $D$ is a
closed convex cone, and we write the convexity step out since the half-space
lemma needs it: additivity gives $\mathbb{N}$-homogeneity, whence
$\mathbb{Q}_+$-homogeneity ($q=m/n$: $n\cdot(qd)=md\in D$ and $n$-fold
additivity on $qd$ give $qd\in D$), and closedness extends this to
$\mathbb{R}_+$-homogeneity; positive homogeneity and additivity together give
convexity---for $d_1,d_2\in D$ and $\lambda\in[0,1]$ one has
$\lambda d_1,(1-\lambda)d_2\in D$ by $\mathbb{R}_+$-homogeneity, hence
$\lambda d_1+(1-\lambda)d_2\in D$ by additivity.
(4) \emph{Half-space lemma}: in the quotient $V:=\mathbb{R}^n/W$, the image
$\bar D$ is a proper closed convex cone with $\bar D\cup(-\bar D)=V$. For
closedness, the quotient map $q$ satisfies $q^{-1}(\bar D)=D+W=D$ because
$W\subseteq D$ and $0\in W$; hence $\bar D$ is closed in the quotient
topology. It is pointed since $q(d),-q(d)\in\bar D$ imply $d\in D\cap(-D)=W$.
If
$\dim V\ge2$, intersect with a 2-plane: a proper closed convex cone covering a
plane must equal a half-plane, but then $\bar D\cap(-\bar D)$ contains a line
$\neq\{0\}$, contradicting \emph{pointedness} of the quotient cone
($\bar D\cap(-\bar D)=\{0\}$ by construction). A half-plane is proper, so
properness itself would not supply the contradiction. Strict coordinatewise monotonicity
excludes $\dim V=0$: otherwise $W=\mathbb{R}^n$, making $g$ constant along
every translation and hence making $F$ constant on $\mathbb{R}_{++}^n$.
Therefore $\dim V=1$: some $w\in\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{0\}$ has
$D=\{d:w\cdot d\ge0\}$.
(5) Therefore $g(x+d)\ge g(x)\iff w\cdot d\ge0$: level sets of $g$ are the
hyperplanes $\{w\cdot x=\mathrm{const}\}$, so $g=\varphi(w\cdot x)$ with
$\varphi$ continuous increasing; strict increase of $F$ gives $w\gg0$.
(6) Let $s:=\sum_iw_i>0$. Homogeneity $F(tx)=tF(x)$ gives
$g(u+t'\mathbf1)=g(u)+t'$ for $t'=\log t$. Substitution into
$g(u)=\varphi(w\cdot u)$ therefore yields the scaled intermediate statement
\[
\varphi(z+st')=\varphi(z)+t',\qquad z=w\cdot u .
\]
Equivalently, for $q=st'$, $\varphi(z+q)=\varphi(z)+q/s$; the slope is $1/s$,
not $1$ before normalization. Define $\tilde w:=w/s$ and
$\tilde\varphi(q):=\varphi(sq)$. Then
$\tilde\varphi(q+t')=\tilde\varphi(q)+t'$. Continuous additivity gives
$\tilde\varphi(q)=q+\tilde\varphi(0)$, and $F(1)=1$ gives
$\tilde\varphi(0)=0$. Hence $g(u)=\tilde w\cdot u$ with
$\sum_i\tilde w_i=1$, i.e.\
$F(x)=\exp(\sum_i\tilde w_i\log x_i)=\prod_ix_i^{\tilde w_i}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Minimality with calibration; zero annihilation and
biseparability as consequences]\label{rem:routeb-minimal}
The proof needs \emph{no} separability axiom: calibration (A0), continuity,
strict monotonicity, coordinatewise scale invariance, and homogeneity
suffice. Consequently, zero annihilation (A4)$=$(AX5) and biseparability
(A3)$=$(AX7) are consequences of this calibrated invariance set, not
independent commitments: the product extends continuously to $[0,1]^n$ with
$F(x)=0\iff\min_ix_i=0$ and separates over every non-trivial partition.
Each condition has a distinct counterexample. Dropping (A0) permits
$c\prod_ix_i^{w_i}$, including $\tfrac12\sqrt{xy}$. Dropping strict
monotonicity permits sub-support products. Dropping (A6) admits the harmonic
family (Proposition~\ref{prop:harmonic}). Dropping homogeneity permits
$F(x)=(1+\prod_ix_i^{w_i})/2$, which satisfies calibration, continuity,
strict monotonicity, and order invariance but is not a weighted geometric
mean. The load-bearing mathematical premises are therefore
$\{\textup{(A0)},\textup{AX4},\textup{AX6b},\textup{AX8}\}$; their
normative defenses are registered separately in Section~\ref{sec:objections}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The harmonic counterexample (Proposition~\ref{prop:harmonic}; Appendix~\ref{app:representation}).}
$H(x)=(\sum_iw_ix_i^{-1})^{-1}$ is quasi-arithmetic with generator
$\phi(x)=-1/x$ (strictly increasing: positive substitutability), continuous,
zero-annihilating, biseparable, and homogeneous; the order reversal
$H(y)=0.5>H(x)=0.4$ yet $H(\lambda\odot y)=0.2<H(\lambda\odot x)=0.25$ for
$x=(0.25,1)$, $y=(0.5,0.5)$, $\lambda=(1,0.25)$ violates (A6). The same
construction with arbitrary $(2,3.5,4)$ power-family witnesses is used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:ax6b}.

\paragraph{CES correspondence.} $F_\varsigma\leftrightarrow$ power mean
$p=(\varsigma-1)/\varsigma$, $\varsigma=1/(1-p)$. Within this preregistered
family, the full conditions admit $\varsigma=1$ only; $\varsigma=2\Rightarrow
p=1/2>0$ violates zero annihilation (Corollary~\ref{cor:sigma-band}). This
family restriction is not used as a derivation of quasi-arithmetic means
from (A1)--(A5).

\section{Component well-definedness}\label{app:components}

For each channel measure of Section~\ref{sec:channels} we show only:
range $[0,1]$, the stated zero semantics, and invariance under the explicitly
registered transformations. These facts do not prove a measurement-theoretic
ratio scale or substantive meaningfulness of channel ratios; AX6a records
that further normative interpretation separately. $T<\infty$ and AX1 are
standing.

\begin{proposition}[$\Avia$, $\Rdef$, $\Astate$]\label{prop:wd-via}
$\Avia=\min(1,\min_k s_k^{\mathrm{avail}}/s_k^{\mathrm{crit}})$ and
$\Rdef=\min(1,\min_jm_j)$ are clipped minima in $[0,1]$, monotone in their
respective resource and defense arguments. $\Avia=0$ iff a critical current
resource is exhausted; $\Rdef=0$ iff a registered defense margin is zero.
Consequently $0\le\Ares=\Rdef\Avia F_\varsigma\le\Astate=\Avia F_\varsigma
\le1$. Zero defense readiness annihilates only the resilience-adjusted
$\Ares$, not necessarily present $\Astate$. The historical combined gate is a
legacy resilience index, not this proposition's current-state gate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[$\Aorg$]\label{prop:wd-org}
Whenever $\Fsrc+\Foth>0$, $\Aorg=\Fsrc/(\Fsrc+\Foth)\in[0,1]$;
the all-zero $0/0$ case is flagged rather than assigned a point. At L0,
$\Aorg=1$ means no residual external flow, including in the pure-register
random example, and is only internal-causal autonomy. At L1+,
$\Aorg=1$ additionally requires $\Frand=0$, while a purely random-register
policy with $\Frand>0$ has $\Aorg=0$. Kramer's chain rule gives the respective
flow decompositions. The ratios are invariant under bijective,
time-structure-preserving relabelings that preserve the registered $N/R/I$
factors; general coarse-grainings or reassigning normative memory to noise can
change every term and are new instantiations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[$\Agoal$]\label{prop:wd-goal}
Assume $\Fix$ is nonempty and closed. Then compact $\Theta$ makes
$\dc=\max_{U\in\Theta}d(U,\Fix)$ attained and finite;
$d(\cdot,\Fix)$ is continuous, so $\Agoal\in[0,1]$ when $\dc>0$, with
$\Agoal=0\iff d=\dc$ (maximally alienated) and $\Agoal=1\iff
\Ueff\in\Fix$. Degenerate regimes are reported, never silently $0/0$: if
$\Fix=\varnothing$ under the tolerance, the answer is the Hausdorff interval
around the non-empty $\varepsilon_{\min}$-approximate fixed-point set,
clipped to $[0,1]$ and flagged. Attainment of $\varepsilon_{\min}$ requires
continuity of the endorsement residual on compact $\Theta$, or an explicitly
finite grid protocol. If $\Fix=\Theta$ (endorsement-blind,
$\dc=0$), the flagged interval is $[0,1]$ and the historical clause is
carried by $\Lam$ alone (the division of labor that keeps
Proposition~\ref{prop:unidentifiable} coherent). The ratio uses one protocol-fixed metric $d$ in numerator and denominator, so
$d/\dc$ is invariant under rescaling $d\mapsto ad$ and $\dc\mapsto a\dc$.
That invariance does \emph{not} prove that $1-d/\dc$ is itself a ratio scale:
$A_{goal}\mapsto aA_{goal}$ is not the transformation induced by the distance
rescaling. Reparameterizations of $\Theta$ beyond scale can change the
reading; the protocol restriction is a registered boundary, not a proof of
ratio meaningfulness (Section~\ref{sec:objections}\textup{(viii)}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[$\Aexec$, precommitted open-loop]\label{prop:wd-exec}
For the open-loop intervention law of \eqref{eq:aexec}, conditional on
$S_t=s$ the sequence $A^n$ is drawn from a precommitted $Q$ and the outcome is
generated by $K_{t,s,n}(\cdot\given s,a^n)$. Hence
$I_Q(A^n;O_{t+n}\given S_t=s)\le H_Q(A^n\given S_t=s)\le n\log|\Acal|$,
so $\Aexec\in[0,1]$. The earlier $A_{t:t+n}$ indexing with denominator
$n\log|\Acal|$ was invalid; the later ordinary-MI form with exactly $n$
actions was also invalid for reactive policies because it counted
observation-to-action feedback. The feedback-copy witness in
Section~\ref{sec:channels} has old score $1/2$ and open-loop score $0$.

For a finite nonempty compact feasible distribution set and fixed kernel,
mutual information is continuous and concave in $Q$, so a maximum exists.
Blahut--Arimoto applies to an explicit finite discrete channel, including a
derived open-loop sequence channel \citep{cover_thomas2006}; variational bounds
have separate premises \citep{mohamed2015}. No attainment or BA claim is made
for an unstructured feedback-policy class.

$\Aexec=0$ in capacity mode iff every sequence that can receive positive
probability under some admissible $Q$ induces the same conditional
terminal-observation kernel (with all pure-sequence point masses admissible,
equivalently: all allowed sequences coincide; equivalently, every admissible
$Q$ has zero MI). For a fixed precommitted $Q$, zero MI holds iff the kernels
coincide on the positive-probability support of $Q$; a point-mass $Q$ is
always zero even when unused rows differ. Repertoire collapse to one sequence
and complete action--environment decoupling are sufficient causes. The
pre-intervention normalizer prevents
$\delta$-contractions from spuriously raising the ratio. Continuous actions
require registered finite quantization or a positive same-protocol capacity
normalizer; $\log\mathrm{Vol}(\Acal^n)$ is not a general MI upper bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[$\Aepi$]\label{prop:wd-epi}
$\bar D$ of \eqref{eq:aepi} is an expectation of TV distances against point
masses, hence in $[0,1]$ with canonical upper bound $1$; $\Aepi^{real}\in[0,1]$.
$\Aepi^{real}=0$ iff $\mathbb{E}[B_t(\kappa(E_t))]=0$ at every $t$: beliefs
put no mass on the true state throughout (epistemic capture as complete
detachment). ENV-0's point-belief specialization replaces this expectation
by $\Pr[b_t=\kappa(E_t)]$ without changing its numerical truth path.
$\Aepi^{real}=1$ iff beliefs are perfectly accurate on the registered
coarse-graining. The ENV-0 baseline value $\Aepi^{real}=0.811$ is a
calibration fact, not an instance of T1c's D1 clause: D1 bounds execution
empowerment, and observation noise confined to coordinates orthogonal to
$\kappa(E)$ need not reduce $\Aepi^{real}$ at all. A general strict epistemic
bound would require a separate registered-coordinate non-degeneracy premise.
Honest
information (the $\alpha$ class) moves beliefs toward the truth and cannot
decrease $\Aepi^{real}$ when the agent's update rule is sound in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:sound-update} (the honesty
sub-classification of Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}); deceptive content and
channel degradation decrease it only under the separately registered
accuracy-loss premise (R-epi), not from those labels alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[Sound update rule]\label{def:sound-update}
Let the belief $B$ range over distributions on the coarse-grained state space
(registered $\kappa$), and let an \emph{honest signal about the registered
coordinate} be an observation $O$ generated by a channel law
$o(\cdot\given E)$ with the true state $E$. An update rule
$U:(B,O)\mapsto B'$ is \emph{sound} (relative to $\kappa$) if honest signals
are weakly accuracy-improving in expectation over the signal law: for every
state $e$ and belief $B$,
\[
\mathbb{E}_{O\sim o(\cdot\given e)}\bigl[\TV\bigl(U(B,O),\,\delta_{\kappa(e)}\bigr)\bigr]
\;\le\; \TV\bigl(B,\,\delta_{\kappa(e)}\bigr),
\]
This weak inequality is the general definition; no strict-if-and-only-if
claim is made for an arbitrary $U$, whose internal prediction need not be
defined. A strictness characterization is proved separately for Bayesian
updating under the likelihood-fidelity and support assumptions of
Lemma~\ref{lem:sound-update}, where the agent predictive $p(o)$ is defined
explicitly. The soundness property is a constraint on the update rule relative to the
\emph{true} channel law; it is the epistemic-channel analogue of a
regularity condition, and its failure modes (misspecified or coarse-grained
update rules acting on off-coordinate honest content) are the registered
boundary clause of Section~\ref{sec:channels}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Bayesian conditioning is sound under local likelihood fidelity]
\label{lem:sound-update}
Let $B$ be a prior over registered states and let the agent use likelihoods
$p(o\given z)$ with predictive marginal $p(o)=\sum_zB(z)p(o\given z)$. For a
true state $e$, write $k=\kappa(e)$ and assume
\textup{(L)} \emph{local likelihood fidelity}: the actual observation law at
$e$ is $q_e(\cdot)=p(\cdot\given k)$. Also assume support coverage,
$p(o)=0\Rightarrow q_e(o)=0$. Then Bayesian updating satisfies
$\mathbb{E}_{q_e}[B'(k)]\ge B(k)$, strictly when $0<B(k)<1$ and
$q_e(\cdot)\neq p(\cdot)$. Since $\TV(B,\delta_k)=1-B(k)$, this is the
soundness condition of Definition~\ref{def:sound-update}. Assumption (L) is
local to the registered coordinate; it does not require a globally correct
model. Support coverage without (L) is not sufficient.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The posterior numerator is the agent's likelihood, not the true channel:
\[
B'(k)=B(k)p(o\given k)/p(o).
\]
Under (L), $q_e(o)=p(o\given k)$, hence
\[
\mathbb{E}_{q_e}[B'(k)]
=B(k)\sum_o\frac{p(o\given k)^2}{p(o)}
\ge B(k)\Bigl(\sum_op(o\given k)\Bigr)^2=B(k),
\]
by Cauchy--Schwarz and $\sum_op(o)=1$. Equality holds iff $p(\cdot\given
k)=p(\cdot)$ on the support, or if $B(k)\in\{0,1\}$. To see why (L) cannot be dropped,
let the true observation be $O=0$ almost surely, $B(k)=1/2$, and set
$p(0\given k)=\varepsilon$, $p(0\given\neg k)=1-\varepsilon$ with
$0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Support coverage holds, but $B'(k)=\varepsilon<B(k)$;
at $\varepsilon=0.1$, TV to the true point mass rises from $0.5$ to $0.9$.
Thus an alternative proof that merely replaces $p(o\given k)$ by the true
channel in the posterior numerator would be invalid.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[$\Aref$ and $\Lam$]\label{prop:wd-ref}
Let $\theta_\pi$ and $M$ range over the protocol-constructed \emph{finite
discrete} ensemble (continuous variables must first undergo a registered
finite quantization). Then discrete Shannon information gives
$0\le I(M;\theta_\pi)\le H(\theta_\pi)$, so fidelity is in $[0,1]$ whenever
$H(\theta_\pi)>0$. $\Aref$ is a product of this factor, endorsement
consistency, and $\Lam_{ref}$, hence lies in $[0,1]$; $H(\theta_\pi)=0$ is
reported as $[0,f_2f_3]$, never a silent $0/0$. Differential entropy supplies
no such bound: for independent $\theta\sim N(0,100)$ and

\begin{lemma}[T2, mediation --- restated]
Under \textup{H-0} and \eqref{eq:factorization}, if $I$ has only
$\alpha/\sigma$ components, then $I^t\ind A_t\given(A^{t-1},S^t)$ for every
$t$, hence $\Fext=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the global Markov property (H-0) it suffices to $d$-separate $I^t$ from
$A_t$ given $(A^{t-1},S^t)$. The only incoming edges to $A_t$ are from $S_t$,
so the penultimate node of any path from $I_k$ ($k\le t$) into $A_t$ lies in
$S_t$ and occurs as a chain or fork node (conditioned on, hence blocked). The
path classes: (i) $\alpha$-paths $I\to O_j\to B_j\to\cdots\to A_t$ cross some
$B_j\in S^t$ (chain/fork, blocked); (ii) $\sigma$-paths via $Z_j\to
E_{j+1}\to O_{j+1}\to B_{j+1}$ cross $B_{j+1}\in S^t$ (blocked); the
alternative branch $Z_t\to E_{t+1}\leftarrow A_t$ terminates in the collider
$E_{t+1}$, which is conditioned on neither directly nor through any
descendant---the conditioning set contains only components of $S^t$, and
$B_{t+1}$ (its descendant via $O_{t+1}$) has index $t+1>t$---so the branch
stays closed; (iii) loop-backs through $A_j$ ($j<t$) are blocked at
$A^{t-1}$; (iv) common-cause paths do not exist ($I$ exogenous). Hence the
conditional independence, and every DI term in $\Fext$ vanishes.

The convention matters: under full-sequence conditioning ($S^T$) the set
contains $B_{t+1}$, opening (ii)'s collider branch and invalidating the
lemma; the claim that ``both conventions work verbatim'' (conditioning only
grows) mistakes the direction of the collider effect---larger conditioning
sets need not preserve $d$-separation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[TV continuity of bounded estimators]\label{lem:tv-continuity}
Let $\hat A=f(\xi)$ with $\xi$ registered evidence (a measurable function of
the internal observation stream) and $\mathrm{range}(f)\subseteq[0,1]$. Then
$|\mathbb{E}_\gamma[\hat A]-\mathbb{E}_0[\hat A]|\le
\TV(P_\xi^\gamma,P_\xi^0)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$|\int f\,dP_\gamma-\int f\,dP_0|\le\sup|f|\cdot\TV\le\TV$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[T3b --- restated]

\textup{(HI-2)} $\hat A=f(\xi)$ with calibration bias
$|\mathbb{E}_0[\hat A]-A^0|\le\eta_0$, \textup{(HI-3)} distortion growth
$A^0-A^\gamma\ge c_1 s(\gamma)^{\nu_0}$ ($c_1>0$, $\nu_0\in\{1,2\}$): in expectation
over the evidence law,
$\mathbb{E}_\gamma[\hat A]\ge\mathbb{E}_0[\hat A]-\varepsilon\ge
A^0-\varepsilon-\eta_0> A^0-c_1s^{\nu_0}\ge A^\gamma$ whenever
$c_1s^{\nu_0}>\varepsilon+\eta_0$, and the expected gap obeys
$\mathbb{E}_\gamma[\hat A]-A^\gamma\ge g(s):=
c_1s^{\nu_0}-\varepsilon-\eta_0$, whose lower bound $g(s)$, not necessarily
the actual expected gap, is strictly increasing in $s$. With
$X:=\hat A-A^\gamma\in[-1,1]$, Markov's inequality gives
$\Pr_\gamma[\hat A>A^\gamma]=\Pr_\gamma[\AHI>0]\ge g(s)$
(vacuous when the right-hand side exceeds $1$; informative range $(0,1]$).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\mathbb{E}_\gamma[\hat A]\ge\mathbb{E}_0[\hat A]-\varepsilon$ by

$\mathbb{E}_0[\hat A]\ge A^0-\eta_0$ by the calibration-period bias bound;
(HI-3) gives $A^\gamma\le A^0-c_1s^{\nu_0}$; the chain and the expected-gap
bound follow. Only the lower bound is strictly monotone, because
$c_1s^{\nu_0}$ is strictly increasing and $\varepsilon,\eta_0$ are
$s$-independent; no monotonicity claim for the actual expectation follows.
For the firing
floor, $X:=\hat A-A^\gamma\in[-1,1]$ with $\mathbb{E}[X]\ge g:=
c_1s^{\nu_0}-\varepsilon-\eta_0>0$ gives

the hypotheses are unsatisfiable ($\mathbb{E}[X]\le1$ always), so the floor
is vacuous there and its informative range is $g\in(0,1]$. All constants
are used as stated
(no constant and its reciprocal are interchanged); the calibration step is an

composed with (A2) and the explicit $A^0>0$ premise. It is not a consequence

\section{Proofs for T4 and T5}\label{app:t4t5}

\begin{proposition}[T4(i), highest-leverage concentration --- restated]
Under geometric aggregation, $A_i^0>0$, fixed $\Avia>0$, and
\textup{(LinLog)}, every full-budget vertex in
$\mathcal I^\star=\arg\max_iw_is_i/c_i$ minimizes $A$; uniqueness holds only
for a singleton $\mathcal I^\star$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Minimizing $A$ is equivalent to maximizing $\sum_iw_is_ix_i$ subject to
$\sum_ic_ix_i\le B$: for feasible $x$,
\[
\sum_iw_is_ix_i\le\max_i(w_is_i/c_i)\sum_i c_ix_i
\le (w_{i^*}s_{i^*}/c_{i^*})B,
\]
with equality at any registered best-leverage vertex. Ties belong to
$\mathcal I^\star$ and may make a whole vertex face optimal.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[T4(ii), defender equalizes --- restated]
With defense $e$ and budget $\sum_ie_i\le E$, $E>0$, let
$\rho_i(e_i)=w_is_i(e_i)/c_i(e_i)$ be continuous and nonincreasing, and assume
that every unique strict maximum is locally strictly decreasing in its
channel's defense. At an optimum of $\min_e\max_i\rho_i(e_i)$, either maximal
funded channels have a common water level $R$ and unfunded channels have
$\rho_i(0)\le R$, or exactly one channel consumes the entire usable budget.
These are necessary, not sufficient, conditions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Existence follows by compactness and continuity. Suppose a unique strict
maximum is locally strictly decreasing and can be reduced by allocating more
defense to its channel using unused budget or a donor. Continuity permits a
transfer small enough that every other ratio stays below the former maximum,
while the maximal ratio strictly falls---a contradiction. If no such resource
exists, exactly one channel is funded with the full usable budget, so equality
over the funded set is vacuous. Otherwise the maximum is tied. An unfunded
channel can enter the active set from zero; it need not equalize smaller
entries. Constant responses $\rho_1\equiv2,\rho_2\equiv1$ show why the
responsiveness premise cannot be dropped: every allocation, including a split
allocation with a unique strict maximum, is then optimal. The condition
$\rho_i(0)\le R$ is merely the maximum definition and is not a sufficiency
claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Linear-fraction attack: correct boundary analysis]
\label{prop:linear-fraction}
For $w_i,c_i,s_i>0$, $B>0$, and $0\le s_ix_i\le1$, minimize
$J(x)=\sum_iw_i\log(1-s_ix_i)$ over $x_i\ge0$ and $\sum_i c_ix_i\le B$.
If zero retention is feasible, the extended minimum $-\infty$ is attained at
a zero boundary, potentially also by non-extreme points. On the strictly
positive-retention feasible domain, a global minimum is attained at an extreme
point, with candidate values $J_i=w_i\log(1-s_iB/c_i)$. Interior KKT
stationarity of the concave objective is not a generic minimum certificate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the strictly positive-retention domain, each nonconstant term is strictly
concave: $\partial_iJ=-w_is_i/(1-s_ix_i)$ and
$\partial_{ii}J=-w_is_i^2/(1-s_ix_i)^2<0$. If $x=\sum_k\alpha_kv_k$ is a
non-extreme convex combination there, then
$J(x)>\sum_k\alpha_kJ(v_k)\ge\min_kJ(v_k)$, so an extreme minimizer exists.
The zero-retention case is separate: for example, at $B=3$ with two unit-cost,
unit-sensitivity channels, $(1,1/4)$ is non-extreme and also has $J=-\infty$,
so no uniqueness or extreme-only claim is made there. Budget monotonicity
reduces positive-retention candidates to the displayed full-budget vertices. For the two-channel symmetric example $w_i=0.5$,
$c_i=s_i=1$, $B=0.5$, the interior point $(0.25,0.25)$ satisfies the
equality-budget KKT condition with multiplier $2/3$ and Hessian $-(8/9)I$,
hence is a local maximum of $J$, while $(0.5,0)$ or $(0,0.5)$ gives the
smaller value $-\tfrac12\log2=-0.3465735903$ instead of $-0.2876820725$.
The R14 grid and symbolic certificates are in
\texttt{sim/analysis\_r14\_math.json}.
\end{proof}

\section{Proofs for T6}\label{app:t6}

Recall the dynamics and mechanisms M1--M4 from Section~\ref{sec:t6} and
assumptions \textup{(H1)}--\textup{(H4$''$)} (additive linearly mapped damage;
units unified by calibration into damage-nats/time, with $\chi_j$-estimation

\begin{theorem}[T6, main theorem --- restated]
See Theorem~\ref{thm:t6}; the margin is two-tier: the pointwise floor
$\Avia^{\min}\prod_j(A_j^{base}(1-\bar\delta_j/\kappa_j))^{w_j}>0$ for
$\bar\delta_j<\kappa_j$, and the uniform floor \eqref{eq:amin}, positive iff
no $\kappa_j$ binds under the standing non-degenerate baseline premises.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) M4 is a deterministic modeling premise, not a random asymptotic. For a
$k$-of-$r$ majority of independent copies, a majority takeover requires
corrupting $k_j=\lceil(r_j+1)/2\rceil$ copies, which at cost $c_{0,j}$ per
copy per window $W_j$ requires budget rate $k_jc_{0,j}/W_j$. The registered
M4 recovery premise stipulates that below that rate no majority is held and
consensus restoration gives $\delta_j^{eff}=0$ in every window. With the
upper comparison then reducing to $\dot D_j\le-\kappa_jD_j$, it follows that
$D_j(t)\to0$ deterministically. No per-window probability, limit parameter,
or stochastic conclusion is invoked.
(ii) With $\delta_j<\theta_j/W_j$, M1 never triggers (window accumulation
$W_j\delta_j<\theta_j$), so M3 is the only active term:
$\dot D_j\le\delta_j-\kappa_jD_j$; the comparison principle (local Lipschitz
right-hand side) gives $\limsup_tD_j(t)\le\delta_j/\kappa_j$, and
$\delta_j<\deltastar_j\le\kappa_j$ makes the bound $<1$.
(iii) Substituting the component map $A_j=A_j^{base}(1-D_j)$ at
$D_j=\bar\delta_j/\kappa_j$ into the product form of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rep}, together with $\Avia\ge\Avia^{\min}$, gives the
pointwise tier. Its strict positivity uses both $\Avia^{\min}>0$ and
$A_j^{base}>0$ for every applicable $j$; omitting either premise would permit
a zero or an overestimated floor. For the uniform tier,
$\deltastar_j<\kappa_j$ gives $1-\deltastar_j/\kappa_j>0$ in every factor,
and $\Avia\ge\Avia^{\min}>0$ by (H4$'$); if $\kappa_j=\deltastar_j$ the
$j$-th factor is $0$---the uniform floor degenerates continuously while the
pointwise tier keeps a positive bound for every fixed
$\bar\delta_j<\kappa_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[T6, registration domain --- restated]

A_j\;\ge\;A_j^{base}(1-\theta_j-\ell_j\delta_j)\;>\;0,
\]

\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Accumulation at rate $\delta_j$ reaches $\theta_j$ within the window; the lag

$A_j=A_j^{base}(1-D_j)$ gives the displayed per-channel bound. No assertion
about an unrelated factor is made.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[T6, Gaussian detection-only lower bound --- restated]
For iid $N(0,s_n^2)\to N(\delta,s_n^2)$ observations, fixed drift
$\delta>0$, and any admissible scheme family $(T_\alpha)$ satisfying
$\mathbb{E}_\infty[T_\alpha]\ge1/\alpha$, let $\mathrm{EDD}_\alpha$ be
Lorden's worst-case delay of $T_\alpha$. Then, family by family,
$\delta\,\mathrm{EDD}_\alpha\ge(1-o(1))2s_n^2\log(1/\alpha)/\delta\to\infty$
as $\alpha\downarrow0$. The conclusion concerns an asymptotic lower bound on
expected cumulative drift damage at fixed nonzero drift, not pointwise
damage at detection, a class-uniform $o(1)$, or a universal weak-drift
asymptotic.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each admissible family, Lorden's lower bound (with Lai's refinement)
gives worst-case $\mathrm{EDD}_\alpha\ge(1-o(1))\log(1/\alpha)/D(\delta)$
as $\alpha\downarrow0$ at fixed $D(\delta)>0$. The Gaussian KL is exactly
$D(\delta)=\delta^2/(2s_n^2)$, so no unjustified local expansion is needed
\citep{lorden1971,lai1995,tartakovsky2014}. Multiplying by the fixed
$\delta$ gives the scheme-family-wise claim. We do not turn the separate
per-family $o(1)$ terms into a uniform bound over a class of schemes.

The former quantifier order---fixed $\alpha$, then
$\delta\downarrow0$---is false over all sequential rules, even after
restricting to Gaussian observations. For any fixed $\alpha\in(0,1)$, the
constant stopping time $T\equiv K$ with $K=\lceil1/\alpha\rceil$ is a legal
rule, satisfies $\mathbb{E}_\infty[T]=K\ge1/\alpha$, and has Lorden delay at
most $K$ independently of $\delta$. Hence $\delta\,\mathrm{EDD}\le\delta K
\to0$ as $\delta\downarrow0$. Nontrivial observation dependence does not
rescue the claim: $T=K+\mathbf 1\{X_1>0\}$ satisfies the same false-alarm
constraint and has delay at most $K+1$. A weak-drift statement would
therefore require a different class of procedures and additional
nontrivial-power or consistency premises, with a separate joint-asymptotic
proof; none is claimed here.

The distribution restriction is also necessary. If $P_0=U[0,1]$ and
$P_\delta=U[\delta,1+\delta]$, the variances agree but
$D(P_\delta\|P_0)=\infty$ for every $\delta>0$. The rule that alarms on the
first $X>1$ has zero pre-change false alarms and $\mathrm{EDD}=1/\delta$, so
$\delta\,\mathrm{EDD}=1$ does not diverge. Hence ``additive drift plus a
noise variance'' alone cannot support the proposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[T6, design rule and certificate-failure band --- restated]
If $\theta_j/W_j\le\kappa_j$, every drift lies in a certificate domain:
$\delta<\theta/W$ gives $\limsup_tD(t)\le\delta/\kappa<1$ through M3, and
$\delta\ge\theta/W$ is registerable by M1. The M1 branch yields per-channel
non-capture or positive component retention only when M1 responds normally and
$\theta+\ell\delta$ is below the capture threshold, as in the registration
proposition above. Also
$\deltastar_j=\min(\theta_j/W_j,\,\lceil(r_j+1)/2\rceil c_{0,j}/W_j)$
(the $\kappa$-term slack). If $\theta/W>\kappa$ on a channel where the
deterministic M4 recovery premise is unavailable, then for fixed

bound is $\limsup_tD(t)\le\delta/\kappa$, with right-hand side greater than

This is a \emph{certificate-failure band}, not a capture theorem. Indeed,

\begin{proposition}[T6, term-wise monotonicity --- restated]
Write $a=\theta/W$, $b=kc_0/W$, and
$\deltastar=\min(a,\kappa,b)$. Holding all other arguments fixed:
\textup{(i)} $\deltastar$ is nondecreasing in $\theta$, in $\kappa$, and
stepwise nondecreasing in $r$ through $k=\lceil(r+1)/2\rceil$, and is
nonincreasing in $W$; it is strictly increasing only when the changed term
binds and actually changes the minimum. \textup{(ii)} The floor
\eqref{eq:amin} is nonincreasing in $\deltastar$ for fixed $\kappa$.
Because $\deltastar/\kappa=\min(a/\kappa,1,b/\kappa)$, it is nondecreasing
in $\kappa$. Consequently, raising $\varepsilon$ or $v$, or shortening
$\tau$ on a gated channel, weakly raises both $\deltastar$ and $\Amin$
through $\kappa$. On a region with a unique minimizer, only that term has a
nonzero local comparative static; ties can produce flat one-sided changes.
On a non-degenerate product domain where $\Avia^{\min}$ and every other
multiplicative factor are positive, the floor rises strictly with $\kappa$
when a positive-weight channel with $A_j^{base}>0$ moves from
$\deltastar_j=\kappa_j$ to $\deltastar_j<\kappa_j$, or when $a_j$ or $b_j$
binds and $\kappa_j$ strictly decreases the ratio $\deltastar_j/\kappa_j$.
Without that domain condition, another identically zero factor can make the
strict claim vacuous.
\textup{(iii)} Raising $\theta$ weakly raises $\deltastar$ but weakly lowers
\eqref{eq:amin}; lowering $\theta$ reverses both directions. Raising $r$
weakly raises $\deltastar$ but, with $\kappa$ fixed, weakly lowers
\eqref{eq:amin}. Neither $\theta$ nor $r$ licenses a joint ``both margins
increase'' claim. The threshold remains double-edged, but the directions
must be stated correctly: raising $\theta$ weakly increases $\deltastar$,
\emph{shrinks} the registerable drift domain $\{\delta:\delta\ge\theta/W\}$,
worsens the per-cycle bound $\theta+\ell\delta$, and weakly lowers the
uniform floor \eqref{eq:amin}. Lowering $\theta$ expands registration
coverage but has the reverse effects on those three quantities; the ARL
constraint imposes $\theta\ge\theta_{\min}(\alpha)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The claims follow from monotonicity of the minimum and of
$1-\deltastar/\kappa$. Two numerical counterexamples fix the previously
misstated directions. With $W=1$, $\kappa=2$, and a slack redundancy term,
lowering $\theta:0.8\to0.4$ lowers $\deltastar:0.8\to0.4$; it cannot raise
the margin. With $A^{base}=1$, $\kappa=10$, $c_0=W=1$, and
$\theta/W\ge2$, raising $r:1\to2$ raises $\deltastar:1\to2$ while a channel
factor of positive weight changes from $0.9^w$ to $0.8^w$, so
\eqref{eq:amin} strictly decreases. For the M4-covered case, use the
separate floor \eqref{eq:amin-m4} below rather than assigning that stronger
mechanism to \eqref{eq:amin}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[T7(i), component-level linear law --- restated]
In the no-innovation special case $\Frand=0$ (hence $\Fsrc=\Fint$ and
$\Foth=\Fext$), under \textup{(H1')} (equal per-step conditional entropy transfer $C$ of both
sources; the script arm bypasses $S$; and $\Fext^{self}=0$---the undelegated
agent carries no residual external flow), the selector-conditional flows
satisfy $\Fint=(1-m)\tilde C$ and $\Fext=m\tilde C$ (same $\tilde C$), hence
$\Aorg(\lambda,q)=\Aorg^{self}(1-\lambda(1-q))$ for that estimand.
Here ``selector-conditional'' means the current-step flows
$I(S^t;A_t\given A^{t-1},\mu_t)$ and
$I(I^t;A_t\given A^{t-1},S^t,\mu_t)$, summed over $t$, not automatically
causal conditioning on the full selector history $\mu^t$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Under the mixture law $(1-m)\pi_{self}+m\pi_{del}$ with the exogenous
selector $\mu_t$ (a node of the extended graph, independent of $S$), internal
flow scales by $(1-m)$ (conditioning on the selector, DPI and the chain
rule); external flow is the selector-gated script content, $m$ times the
script's entropy transfer; (H1') equates the two rates; and with
$\Fext^{self}=0$ the self-side ratio is $\Aorg^{self}=\tilde
C/(\tilde C+0)=1$-normalized, so the ratio is exactly $1-m$. With
$\Fext^{self}=f>0$ the same bookkeeping gives
$\Aorg=(1-m)\tilde C/((1-m)\tilde C+m\tilde C+f)$, a strict correction; the
bracket below absorbs the uncalibrated case.
\emph{The slack bound, explicitly.} For any $X,Y,Z,C$, two applications of
the mutual-information chain rule give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selector-chain}
I(X;Y\given Z,C)-I(X;Y\given C)
=I(Z;Y\given X,C)-I(Z;Y\given C).
\end{equation}
Both terms on the right lie in $[0,H(Z\given C)]\subseteq[0,H(Z)]$, so the
absolute difference is at most $H(Z)$. Taking $X=S^t$, $Y=A_t$, $Z=\mu_t$,
and $C=A^{t-1}$ gives the internal-flow identity
\[
I(S^t;A_t\given A^{t-1},\mu_t)-I(S^t;A_t\given A^{t-1})
=I(\mu_t;A_t\given A^{t-1},S^t)-I(\mu_t;A_t\given A^{t-1}),
\]
whose absolute value is at most $h(m)$. Taking instead $X=I^t$, $Y=A_t$,
$Z=\mu_t$, and $C=(A^{t-1},S^t)$ gives the external-flow identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
&I(I^t;A_t\given A^{t-1},S^t,\mu_t)-I(I^t;A_t\given A^{t-1},S^t)\\
&\quad=I(\mu_t;A_t\given A^{t-1},S^t,I^t)
-I(\mu_t;A_t\given A^{t-1},S^t),
\end{aligned}
\]
with the same $h(m)$ bound. The selector-conditional internal rate is
$(1-m)C_{self}$ under (H1'), so each unconditioned per-step flow deviates
from its registered nominal term by at most $h(m)$ nats, accumulating to at
most $T h(m)$ over the horizon. The ratio $\Aorg$ therefore deviates from
the displayed law by $O\bigl(h(m)/((1-m)C_{self})\bigr)$ per step: the
slack is an $O(1)$ term in rate units, vanishing as per-step transfer grows,
not as $T$ grows. Exactness requires the estimator itself to condition on
$\mu_t$; recording $\mu_t$ but computing the unconditioned left-hand side
does not remove the corrections. The benchmark's legacy DEL-1 implementation
uses unconditioned flows, so its near-linear fit is an ENV-0 numerical
observation, not a verification of the selector-conditional exact law.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[T7(ii), two-error bracket --- restated]\label{prop:bracket}
Use the normalized quantities $a=1-m$,
$\rho=C_{del}/C_{self}\in[\rho_{\min},\rho_{\max}]$,
$\varphi_0=f/C_{self}$, and $\eta_\mu=h(m)/C_{self}$. If the actual internal
and external flows carry independent errors $u,v$ with
$|u|,|v|\le\eta_\mu$, their ratio is $(a+u)/(a+u+m\rho+\varphi_0+v)$. On the
nondegenerate domain of nonnegative flows and positive denominators, the
sharp box-constrained bounds are $[L,U]$ in \eqref{eq:t7-bracket}: minimize
the numerator and maximize the external flow for $L$, and do the reverse for
$U$, finally optimizing $\rho$ over its calibration interval. If
$\varphi_0\in[\varphi_-,\varphi_+]$ is also uncertain, use $\varphi_+$ in $L$
and $\varphi_-$ in $U$. Selector-conditional estimation has $\eta_\mu=0$;
unconditioned estimation has $\eta_\mu\le\ln2/C_{self}$ per step, with the
same ratio valid in horizon units.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $N,D\ge0$ and $N+D>0$, $R=N/(N+D)$ increases in $N$ and decreases in $D$.
Thus $N_{\min}=(a-\eta_\mu)_+$ and
$D_{\max}=m\rho_{\max}+\varphi_0+\eta_\mu$ give $L$, while
$N_{\max}=a+\eta_\mu$ and
$D_{\min}=(m\rho_{\min}+\varphi_0-\eta_\mu)_+$ give $U$. The nonnegative
parts are exactly the zero-flow boundary conditions. Unknown $\varphi_0$ is
handled by its worst endpoint on each side. No identity permits a single
$\varphi$ added only to the denominator to absorb both errors.

For a numerical check of that last point, take $m=0.1$,
$C_{self}=C_{del}=1$, $\varphi_0=0$, and
$\eta_\mu=h(0.1)\approx0.325083$. The former relaxed lower end is
$0.679203$, but $u=-0.25$ and $v=+0.25$ are feasible and yield
$R=0.65/1=0.65$. The corrected endpoints are $L\approx0.574917$ and $U=1$.
This algebraic example refutes the old derivation; by itself it does not
assert that every delegation model realizes these errors jointly.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[T7(iv), structural mediation and conditional losslessness
--- restated]
At full transparency, reversibility, and endorsability ($q=1$):
\textup{(a)} delegate-originated influence is mediated $\alpha$-class
influence, so its bypass flow is zero (and total $\Fext=0$ if the self-side
residual is zero), with no additional $\Aorg$ loss under the same
bookkeeping; \textup{(b)} if, in addition, content is honest on the
registered coordinates, updating is sound, there is no registered-coordinate
accuracy loss, and all other channels and $\Avia$ are unchanged, then
$A(\lambda,1)=A_{self}$. These are sufficient premises for the claimed
no-loss guarantee, not necessary conditions for equality in every model.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

information, without presuming that it is honest; reversibility means no
persistent $\Delta G$ bypasses $\Endorse$; endorsability gives the
registered no-surgery premise. Lemma~\ref{lem:mediation} then removes the
delegate-attributable residual flow. For (b), the additional premises give
$\Aorg=\Aorg^{self}$ and leave every remaining factor equal to its self
baseline, so the product is unchanged.

The structural premises alone do not suffice for the no-loss guarantee. Let
the true registered bucket be $1$,
and let a delegate transparently and reversibly transmit the false but
endorsable message ``the bucket is $0$.'' If the agent registers this false
message as $B'=\delta_0$ rather than the self-baseline

a counterexample to mediation: $\Fext^{del}=0$ while $\Aepi$, and hence the
composite, falls. Thus $q=1$ alone cannot imply full-composite
losslessness.
\end{proof}

\section{Dependence and copula bounds}\label{app:copula}

\begin{lemma}[Jensen gap]\label{lem:jensen-gap}
$F=\prod_ix_i^{w_i}$ ($\sum_iw_i=1$) is concave on $\mathbb{R}_+^n$
(Cobb--Douglas; Hessian negative semidefinite), so for random component
vectors $\mathbf{A}$: $\mathbb{E}[F(\mathbf{A})]\le F(\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}])$
---substituting expected components in black-box/interval mode overestimates
$A$ by a signed amount.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Log-normal exact ratio]
If $\log A_i$ are jointly normal (means $\mu_i$, variances $\sigma_i^2$,
correlations $\rho_{ij}$):
$\mathbb{E}[F]/F(\mathbb{E}[A])=
\exp\bigl(-\tfrac12\bigl[\sum_iw_i\sigma_i^2-\sum_{ij}w_iw_j\rho_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j\bigr]\bigr)\le1$
(Cauchy--Schwarz). Direct MGF computation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Bounds with unknown dependence]\label{prop:copula-bounds}
$F$ coordinatewise increasing and supermodular
($\partial^2F/\partial x_i\partial x_j=w_iw_jF/(x_ix_j)>0$): (i) the upper
bound of $\mathbb{E}[F]$ is attained by the comonotone copula
(supermodular ordering; \citealp{tchen1980,muller_stoyan2002}); (ii) for
$n=2$ the (sharp) lower bound is obtained by integrating against the
Fr\'echet--Hoeffding lower bound $\max(0,F_1+F_2-1)$, which is the
countermonotone copula. For $n\ge3$ the Fr\'echet--Hoeffding lower-bound
form $\max(0,\sum_iF_i-(n-1))$ is \emph{not a copula} (it fails
$n$-increasingness), so it is not attained by any joint distribution: the
integral against it is only a relaxed, generally unattainable lower bound.
A valid, generally non-sharp lower bound is available instead: since
$\log F=\sum_iw_i\log A_i$ is additive in the coordinates, Jensen's
inequality for the convex exponential gives
\[
\mathbb{E}[F]\;\ge\;\prod_i\bigl(\exp\mathbb{E}[\log A_i]\bigr)^{w_i}
\;=\;\prod_i \mathrm{G}(A_i)^{w_i},
\]
where $\mathrm{G}(A_i):=\exp\mathbb{E}[\log A_i]$ is the geometric mean of
the marginal of $A_i$. Jensen equality requires
$\sum_iw_i\log A_i$ to be almost surely constant; with fixed nondegenerate
marginals, generally no copula attains it. The lower end is therefore
computable but generally not attained. With marginals known and dependence
unknown, $\mathbb{E}[F]$ has computable valid bounds---a comonotone upper
end and the generally non-sharp Jensen lower end (or the sharper
countermonotone value when $n=2$)---the dependence-theoretic complement of
the black-box rules. Nested CES within
clusters is only a sensitivity candidate: the full nested map must separately
satisfy calibration, monotonicity, homogeneity, and (A6); (A3) alone does not
establish that compatibility. Nested geometric aggregation is a compatible
special case.
\end{proposition}

\section{Identifiability}\label{app:identifiability}

\begin{proposition}[Tiered identification --- restated]
\textup{(i)} $\Aorg,\Aepi,\Avia$ are registered joint-law functionals; L1+
source attribution requires the $N/R/I$ factorization, not merely an
aggregated $S$ log. \textup{(ig)} $\Agoal$ is point-identified in
structural-read mode when $\Ueff$, the endorsement mechanism, metric, and
nonempty closed $\Fix$ are available with $\dc>0$. \textup{(ib)} In
behavior-inversion mode, the general identified object is the IRL admissible
set and its induced $\Agoal$ interval; singleton identification needs the
stated extra assumptions. \textup{(ii)} $\Aexec$ and $\Aref$ are identified
only conditional on accessible-class and ensemble protocols and their
regularity conditions. \textup{(iii)} $\Lam$ requires a structural model
supporting abduction; interventions on the joint law alone do not identify
cross-world output laws. Thus the unrestricted sentence ``every channel estimand is
point identified'' is withdrawn, not patched by silently inserting $\Agoal$
into a law-functional tier.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{The certified bound (one-sided, conditionally).} See
Proposition~\ref{prop:certified-bound} and \eqref{eq:aorg-interval}:
Lemma~\ref{lem:probe}
makes $F_{ext}^{obs}=I(I^T;A^T)$ observable without $S$-access, and
Remark~\ref{rem:probe-bridge} supplies the bridge to the causally conditioned
estimand: under premise (i) (the bypass premise $I^t\ind S^t\given A^{t-1}$)
the probe MI is
a lower estimate of $\Fext$, so---$\Aorg$ being decreasing in
$\Fext$---the rule's upper end is the certified bound under premise (ii)
($\Fsrc$ drift $\le\eta$; insufficient $\eta$ as negative control). At L1+
the certified upper end conservatively drops nonnegative $\Frand$ from the
denominator; a reference lower end additionally needs a registered random-flow
cap and remains uncertified. Both
premises are stated in the proposition itself because both are black-box
unverifiable in general; the bound is \emph{conditionally} certified. The
lower end treats
$F_{ext}^{obs}$ as exact and is \emph{not} certified: the truth can fall
below it when the external flow is understated, so the rule is a one-sided
bound with a reference lower end, not an interval-containment statement.
Certified scope:
bypass-semantics exogenous randomized probes---\emph{not} non-adversarial
influence in general, since mediation probes (the $\alpha$ class) reverse the
direction. The adaptive-adversary failure mode is calibrated by the
benchmark's BB-2 arm---see the scope paragraph in
Section~\ref{sec:blackbox}.

\paragraph{$\Aexec$: no general passive one-sided bound.}
Probed mode (experimenter-forced $p(a^n)$ sequences) needs only the
action--observation interface. With independent probes ($A\ind S$),
$I(A;O)\le I(A;O\given S)$. Passive policy-driven observation has no general
direction, since
\[
I(A;O\given S)-I(A;O)=I(A;S\given O)-I(A;S).
\]

For a counterexample to the former passive claim, let $S$ be a fair bit and
$N$ an independent biased bit, and let the certified passive policy be
$A=S\oplus N$. Let the environment hold a legitimate copy $E=S$ and output
$O=A$ when $S=0$ and $O=1-A$ when $S=1$. Then $O=N$. Marginally $A$ and $O$
are independent, so $I(A;O)=0$, whereas $I(A;O\given S)=H(N)>0$. State
conditioning therefore increases information. The ENV-0 passive inequality
is retained only as an instance-level descriptive result; otherwise the
black-box direction is unidentified.

\begin{proposition}[$\Lam$ black-box unidentifiable --- restated]
See Proposition~\ref{prop:unidentifiable}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix identical passive $(O^T,A^T)$ and $\Aphen$ laws. In the endogenous model,
a fair exogenous bit $U$ gives endorsement $Y=U$ with no output-relevant
surgery, so actual and no-surgery output laws agree and $\Lam=1$. In the
implanted model, $Y=U\oplus J$ with $J=1$; factual $Y$ remains fair, hence all
specified passive and factual endorsement laws are identical. From factual
$(Y,J)$, the factual abduction context is $C=(Y,J)$, with
$P_{(y,j)}=\delta_y$ and $P^{\varnothing}_{(y,j)}=\delta_{y\oplus j}$.
Abduction recovers $U=Y\oplus J$. Action removes $J$, and prediction with the
same abducted noise gives $Y^0=U$. For $J=1$, the actual output $1-U$ and
counterfactual output $U$ are opposite point masses, so output-law TV $=1$
and $\Lam=0$. Both endorsement processes are nondegenerate across contexts;
no identity-endorsement certification convention is invoked. This also gives
an observationally equivalent pair of SCMs whose counterfactual values differ.
Corollary~\ref{cor:logs-necessary} follows only for the specified passive
protocol: active behavioral probes remain a separate candidate evidence route.
\end{proof}
