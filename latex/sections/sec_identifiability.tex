\section{Identifiability and Black-Box Degradation}\label{sec:identifiability}

Measurement claims are only as good as their identifiability analysis. This
section fixes what is knowable about autonomy, from what data, and with which
degradation rules when internal access is denied. Proofs are in
Appendix~\ref{app:identifiability}.

\subsection{White-box identification}

\begin{proposition}[Tiered identification; goal point values are conditional]
\label{prop:whitebox}
White-box access does not make every estimation mode point-identified.
\emph{Tier 1 (registered law functionals)}: $\Aorg$, $\Aepi$, and $\Avia$ are
joint-law functionals once the source partition is registered. L0 requires
$S/I$; L1+ additionally requires the $N=(G,B,M)$ versus innovation-$R$
factorization. Aggregated $S$ logs without that factorization identify only
the legacy internal-causal quantity, not \eqref{eq:aorgnorm}.
\emph{Tier 1g (structural goal read)}: if $\Ueff$ is a registered structural
variable, the endorsement mechanism and protocol metric are known, and the
fixed-point premises of Section~\ref{sec:channels} hold, then $\Agoal$ is
point-identified (or a flagged degenerate interval). This is a model read,
not an IRL result.
\emph{Tier 1b (behavioral goal inversion)}: from behavior alone, MaxEnt-IRL
generally identifies an admissible set $\mathcal U(P)$, not a point. $\Agoal$
is therefore the induced set/interval of Section~\ref{sec:channels}; point
identification requires the additional exact-case assumptions stated there.
\emph{Tier 2 (protocol-constructed modal objects)}: $\Aexec$ quantifies over
the probed accessible policy class and $\Aref$ over the registered finite
ensemble. Under those constructions their values are unique when the
respective regularity/non-degeneracy conditions hold; without them the objects
are undefined, not secretly point-identified.
\emph{Tier 3 (cross-world counterfactual)}: $\Lam$ requires the structural
local laws and exogenous-noise terms supporting abduction--action--prediction,
strictly stronger than joint-law access with interventions. The MI ratio is
descriptive only.

Directed-information plug-ins carry finite-sample bias; continuous
$k$-NN/neural estimators concern unnormalized information functionals
\citep{kraskov2004,belghazi2018}, not the finite-discrete $\Aref$ ratio.
Blahut--Arimoto applies only to the explicit finite discrete memoryless
channel reduction of Proposition~\ref{prop:wd-exec}; otherwise empowerment
requires a registered closed-loop optimizer or bounds
\citep{mohamed2015,cover_thomas2006}. Offline change-point methods concern the
registration layer \citep{truong2020,salge2013}.
\end{proposition}

Two per-channel notes complement the tier statement.
$\Aexec$ is exact only under the probe protocol that certifies the accessible
class (Section~\ref{sec:blackbox}).
$\Aref$'s measurement ensemble is \emph{constructed} by the protocol
(self-prediction tasks under the perturbation family): invariance of the
reading under admissible protocol re-designs is a reporting obligation, not
an automaticity. And the MI ratio for $\Lam$ is not generally identified with the
counterfactual estimand: in a single deployment with a constant surgery
history it can degenerate to $0$, while history variation alone still does
not identify cross-world output laws. I therefore do not designate a certified
MI-proxy domain (Section~\ref{sec:datareq}).

The measurement protocol is four-phase: a \emph{baseline period} records all
channel parameters and logs without intervention; a \emph{calibration period}
applies known interventions of known type and strength (under ethical review)
and fits the five channel response functions---this is also where unit
conversions (H2), the weights $w_i$ (by one of the three non-circular
determination protocols of Section~\ref{sec:representation}), the drift
tolerance $\eta$, and the strength ratio $\rho$ are estimated; the \emph{measurement
period} probes the components and composites $A$ with the elasticity
robustness band ($\varsigma$); a \emph{longitudinal design} repeats measurement to estimate
$dA/dt$ and the C1/C2 dynamical parameters.

\noindent The $\Ustar$ elicitation protocol (human substrate) is minimally
specified as \emph{structured deliberation}: a recorded sequence of pairwise
comparisons over the canonical parameter box $\Theta$ with the anchored
normalization $U(x_0)=0$, a fixed protocol metric $d$, and an auditable
decision record. Absent this record, $\Agoal$ degrades to the flagged
interval of \eqref{eq:agoal} rather than to a point value.

\subsection{Black-box degradation rules}\label{sec:blackbox}

Without internal access, only behavior--intervention responses are available.
What survives is stated with its certified scope: the certified direction of
each rule is a theorem under an explicit premise; the uncertified directions
are reported as heuristics and labeled as such.

\paragraph{$\Aorg$: a conditionally certified one-sided bound with a
reference lower end.}
By the randomized-probe lemma
(Lemma~\ref{lem:probe}, Appendix~\ref{app:prelim}), an exogenous randomized
probe makes $F_{ext}^{obs}=I(I^T;A^T)$ observable without access to $S$
(the lemma identifies the probe MI with the \emph{unconditioned} directed
information; the bypass premise of the proposition below is what promotes
the comparison to the causally conditioned estimand). The rule, with its two
premises stated \emph{in} the claim rather than around it:
\begin{proposition}[Conditionally certified upper bound for
$\Aorg$]\label{prop:certified-bound}
Assume \textup{(i)} bypass probe semantics, $I^t\ind S^t\given A^{t-1}$
(the probe is exogenous, non-anticipative, and acts outside the
belief-mediation path); and \textup{(ii)} registered-source-flow drift bounded by the
calibration-estimable tolerance, $\Fsrc\le\Fsrc^{base}(1+\eta)$,
$\eta\in[0,1]$. Then, with $F_{ext}^{obs}=I(I^T;A^T)$ the observable probe
flow,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aorg-interval}
\Aorg\;\le\;
\min\Bigl(1,\frac{\Fsrc^{base}(1+\eta)}{\Fsrc^{base}(1+\eta)+F_{ext}^{obs}}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Both premises are black-box unverifiable in general; the bound is therefore
\emph{conditionally} certified, and the BB-2 arm of
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} calibrates exactly what a premise violation
costs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Under (i), $I(I^t;S^t\given A^{t-1})=0$, so the per-term identity displayed
in the scope paragraph below is non-negative termwise and
$F_{ext}^{obs}\le\Fext$. At L1+,
$\Aorg=\Fsrc/(\Fsrc+\Frand+\Fext)\le\Fsrc/(\Fsrc+\Fext)$ because
$\Frand\ge0$; at L0 the same expression has $\Frand=0$. Monotonicity and (ii)
then give \eqref{eq:aorg-interval}.
Remark~\ref{rem:probe-bridge} gives the bridge in full.
\end{proof}
\noindent The upper end of \eqref{eq:aorg-interval} is reported together with
the \emph{reference lower end}
$\Fsrc^{base}(1-\eta)/\bigl(\Fsrc^{base}(1-\eta)+F_{ext}^{obs}\bigr)$,
whose status is categorically different: it treats $F_{ext}^{obs}$ as exact
and implicitly sets $\Frand=0$. At L1+ a displayed reference must instead use
a registered $r_{\max}\ge\Frand$ in
$\Fsrc^{base}(1-\eta)/(\Fsrc^{base}(1-\eta)+r_{\max}+F_{ext}^{obs})$.
Since $F_{ext}^{obs}$ may understate the true flow, even that reference is not
a certified lower bound; \eqref{eq:aorg-interval} is never an
interval-containment result. (Insufficient $\eta$ serves as the negative
control on premise (ii).) The band's
width's dependence on baseline length and $\eta$ is the information content
assessment; certified-direction results are reported in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} (zero violations of the upper end in $3/3$
bypass arms, with widths reported).

\paragraph{Scope of the bound (the two premises, stated as obligations).}
(i) \emph{Probe semantics, and the direction of the bias}: the observable
$I(I^T;A^T)$ and the target
$\Fext=\sum_t I(I^t;A_t\given A^{t-1},S^t)$ differ in general. The per-term
identity
\[
I(I^t;A_t\given A^{t-1},S^t)-I(I^t;A_t\given A^{t-1})
= I(I^t;S^t\given A^{t-1},A_t)-I(I^t;S^t\given A^{t-1})
\]
shows that no direction holds in general. Two probe semantics exhaust the
audit practice. \emph{Bypass-semantics probes}---exogenous, non-anticipative,
and orthogonal to the agent's state given the action history,
$I^t\ind S^t\given A^{t-1}$ (a probe that acts outside the belief-mediation
path, as in the $\beta_\pi$-style script arms of BB-1): the second term of the
identity vanishes and every per-term difference is non-negative, so
$F_{ext}^{obs}\le\Fext$---the raw probe MI is then a \emph{lower} estimate of
the causally conditioned external flow, and the upper end of
\eqref{eq:aorg-interval} is certified. \emph{Mediation probes}---content
absorbed through $B\in S$, i.e.\ the
$\alpha$ class---reverse the direction: with $S=I\oplus N$, $A=S$ one has
$\Fext=0$ while $I(I;A)=0.531$~bit, so the plug-in \emph{over}estimates the
external flow and the certificate simply does not apply---its bypass premise
fails on this family (the truth lies above the plug-in band). The certified
scope of \eqref{eq:aorg-interval} is therefore the bypass family---not
``non-adversarial influence'' in general, since honest $\alpha$ influence is
mediated by construction. The BB-1 anchors (zero upper-end violations in
$3/3$ bypass arms) exercise
bypass arms and do not cover the mediation case. (ii) \emph{Adversarial
adaptation}: the bypass premise requires $I$ exogenous, non-anticipative, and
state-orthogonal; an adaptive adversary whose injection timing correlates
with the agent's state violates it, and the naive exogenous plug-in
misestimates the flow. The benchmark's BB-2 arm calibrates exactly this
failure---the certified bound's premise is violated in $3/3$ adaptive arms,
the naive exogenous plug-in then overestimates the external flow by $+0.17$
to $+0.90$ nats, and the bound no longer holds there; exogenous control arms
retain it. The demonstrated adaptive arms therefore void this
exogenous-probe certificate; they do not prove that every adaptive audit
requires full causal logs. Redesigned probes, trusted provenance, active
mechanism probes, and structural logs are distinct routes requiring separate
assumptions and evaluation.

\paragraph{$\Aexec$: passive direction unidentified; probe direction conditional.}
For the raw passive proxy, no universal inequality between
$I(A;O)$ and $I(A;O\given S)$ exists:
\[
I(A;O\given S)-I(A;O)=I(A;S\given O)-I(A;S),
\]
and the right-hand side can have either sign. Hence passive observation does
\emph{not} generally imply $\Aexec^{obs}\ge\Aexec$; black-box reports must say
\emph{direction unidentified} unless a further structural assumption is
proved. Under an independent probe ($A\ind S$), $I(A;S)=0$, so
$I(A;O)\le I(A;O\given S)$ and the one-sided direction is valid. BB-1's
passive inequality is only an ENV-0 instance observation, not a general
bound (Appendix~\ref{app:identifiability}).

\paragraph{$\Lam$: impossibility as a theorem.}

\begin{proposition}[Observationally equivalent endorsement SCMs are
black-box indistinguishable]\label{prop:unidentifiable}
There exist two nondegenerate structural models with identical specified
passive $(O^T,A^T)$ and $\Aphen$ laws and identical fair factual endorsement
laws, but $\Lam(\mathcal M_{endo})=1$ and $\Lam(\mathcal M_{impl})=0$.
Consequently no functional of that passive protocol identifies $\Lam$ or the
separate surgery-history certificate. This statement does not say that active
mechanism probes must be non-behavioral; it says that \emph{additional}
provenance or mechanism evidence is required.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Let $U$ be a fair bit and fix the same passive behavior in both models. The
endogenous-output model uses $Y=U$ with no output-relevant surgery and has
$\Lam=1$. The implanted-output model uses $Y=U\oplus J$ with $J=1$ and has
nonempty surgery history. Its factual $Y$ is again fair, so the specified
observable laws agree. The factual abduction context is $C=(Y,J)$:
$P_{(y,j)}=\delta_y$ and $P^{\varnothing}_{(y,j)}=\delta_{y\oplus j}$.
Abduction from $(Y,J)$ recovers $U=Y\oplus J$; action sets $J=0$;
shared-noise prediction yields counterfactual output $U$. For $J=1$, actual
and no-surgery outputs are opposite point masses, hence output-law TV $=1$ and
$\Lam=0$. No degenerate-endorsement certification convention is used. The
observation-equivalence of identity and XOR structural models also shows why
joint laws and interventions alone need not identify cross-world
counterfactuals. Appendix~\ref{app:identifiability} and
\texttt{sim/runs/20261116\_r14/x5c\_stratified/} give the executable witness.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Additional provenance or mechanism evidence is necessary;
full logs are one route]\label{cor:logs-necessary}
(i) A certificate of historical endorsement cannot be identified from the
specified passive behavioral protocol alone. It requires some additional
provenance or mechanism evidence: for example supply-chain attestations,
trusted deployment records, active mechanism probes, or structural causal
logs. Proposition~\ref{prop:unidentifiable} does not rank these alternatives
or rule any of them out. (ii) Under H-0, a specified structural model,
logged local mechanisms, and the exogenous information needed for
counterfactual abduction, full causal logs provide \emph{one sufficient
route} to compute $\Lam$; logs alone, without those model assumptions, are
not sufficient. Thus full causal logging is neither proved uniquely
necessary nor claimed to be the only audit architecture. Black-box mode
reports $\Lam\in[\Lam_{\min},1]$, with a nontrivial lower end only when such
extra evidence warrants it (cf.\ \citealp{capability_permission2026}).
\end{corollary}

A mediation-analysis alternative (natural direct effects
\citep{pearl2001}: $\Fext=0\iff\mathrm{NDE}(I\to A)=0$) requires sequential
ignorability \citep{vanderweele2015} and cross-world
counterfactuals---assumptions strictly stronger than the certified-bound
route; we take \eqref{eq:aorg-interval} as primary and NDE as a sensitivity
direction (numerics pending; Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
