\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous systems are increasingly deployed to act persistently on behalf of
their principals: LLM-based agents that plan, remember, and use tools
\citep{wang2023,xi2023,park2023}; recommender and notification systems that
continuously shape what users see; rehabilitation and coaching devices that
nudge human habits. In each deployment the same two questions arise:
\emph{how much autonomy does the agent retain under a given influence}, and
\emph{how much did a given interaction take away}. Existing literatures
operationalize parts of both questions, but lack the proposed shared audit
schema. Governance frameworks grade agents by levels and
profiles \citep{feng2025,kasirzadeh2025,dung2024}; psychology measures
motivation and control \citep{ryan_deci2000,maier_seligman2016}; information
theory measures control loops \citep{klyubin2005,massey1990}; AI safety
catalogues failure modes one by one
\citep{amodei2016,everitt2021,greshake2023}. What is missing is a shared
audit schema: an instantiation-relative operationalization of autonomy,
decomposed into auditable channels and governed by explicit axioms and
theorems rather than unarticulated conventions. This gap is narrower than a
claim that prior work measured only capability and cost: code inspection
\citep{cihon2025}, real deployment oversight measurements
\citep{anthropic2026agents}, bidirectional plasticity--empowerment analysis
\citep{abel2025plasticity}, text-trajectory empowerment estimation
\citep{song2026eelma}, evaluation catalogues
\citep{lassiter2026compendium}, and auditability frameworks
\citep{nian2026auditable} are direct precursors. The schema is
substrate-neutral; its instruments and scalar readings are not automatically
comparable across substrates.

Four misconceptions stand in the way of such a measure, and we dispel them at
the outset. Autonomy is \emph{not isolation}: a closed system is dead, not
free; adaptation requires informational intake (our Axiom~AX2). Autonomy is
\emph{not randomness}: to be autonomous is to be determined by one's self, not
by nothing (Axiom~AX0 makes directed information from internal states to
actions the defining positive quantity). Autonomy is \emph{not omnipotence}:
capability is one channel among five, and its absence does not annihilate the
others. And autonomy is \emph{not the absence of relations}: influences divide
into constitutive support (our $\sigma$ class) and reductive interference;
``zero relations'' is not the maximum of autonomy but lies outside its feasible
region \citep{mackenzie_stoljar2000}.

\paragraph{The proposal.} We define the autonomy of an agent relative to an
environment and a time horizon as the degree to which its behavior is
determined by its own normative structure $(G,B,M)$---its goals, beliefs, and
self-model---in five respects: \emph{direct-bypass origination} (behavior is
not externally driven around the registered decision state), \emph{informedness}
(environment accuracy is distinguished from influence-source and intervention
registration), \emph{endorsability} (second-order review is assessed separately
from tampering history and actual/no-surgery endorsement output deviation),
\emph{executability} (intent
has predictable leverage on the situation, with exit options), and
\emph{viability} (the resource reproduction sustaining all of the above is not
interrupted). These respects map many-to-many onto five channels, each with
an estimator, a protocol normalizer, and a zero semantics; ratio
interpretations remain separate normative commitments rather than proved scale
facts. Reflection has
no separate respect of its own, and viability is a gate rather than a sixth
channel. The primary output is the channel profile and evidence grade. The
secondary scalar composite is comparable \emph{within a layer at a fixed
instantiation}; cross-layer and cross-substrate readings remain profiles
(Section~\ref{sec:framework}).

\paragraph{A note on objectivity, at the outset.} Any measure of autonomy is
built from indicators, and every indicator carries its designer's limitations;
no instrument family is neutral. We therefore do not claim that our channels
\emph{are} autonomy, nor that subjectivity can be designed away. What can be
built is \emph{procedural objectivity}: every design commitment is registered
and priced (including what is lost if it is rejected); invariance under
irrelevant re-instrumentation is itself made an axiom; the boundary between
the knowable and the unknowable is proved rather than assumed; and the
channel choice itself is exposed to a registered external falsifier. The
resulting claim is procedural and conditional auditability: conventions are
explicit, particular invariances and identifiability limits are proved, and
sensitivity is reported. Stability under reconstruction by independent teams
has not been tested (Section~\ref{sec:objectivity}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
The primary contribution is the \emph{audit schema} itself---a measurement
and identifiability discipline for autonomy under influence; the theorems
are its load-bearing instruments, not a claim to a finished mathematical
theory of autonomy.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A formal framework} (Section~\ref{sec:framework}): a
time-unfolded causal graph with a five-class intervention taxonomy
($\sigma/\alpha/\beta/\gamma/\delta$) whose primitive coverage is proved
relative to that interface (Proposition~\ref{thm:taxonomy-completeness}),
with composite labels where one surgery changes several couplings:
within the graph's surgery loci, every external influence acts by changing
what the agent sees, its mechanism itself, what it can do, or its conditions
of self-maintenance. The $\alpha/\gamma$ boundary is a structural criterion
(the intact channel survives as a component, or not), and the one deliberate
boundary---surgeries on the environment's own dynamics---is registered as
re-instantiation of the measurement, not a sixth class.
\item \textbf{Measures from axioms} (Sections~\ref{sec:axioms} and
\ref{sec:representation}): six channel measures, each with an absolute zero and
a canonical normalizer; and a representation theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:rep})
showing that the calibration $F(1,\ldots,1)=1$, continuity, strict
coordinatewise monotonicity, homogeneity, and coordinatewise scale
invariance of the induced order admit exactly the weighted geometric
mean---``forced'' relative to axioms that are defended normative
commitments, not measurement-theoretic necessities (the scale-invariance
characterization itself is classical: \citealp{ebert_welsch2004}; our additions
are scoped in Section~\ref{sec:representation}). One normalization-explicit
proof is used; we do not claim that (A1)--(A3) alone yield a
quasi-arithmetic mean. Harmonic and multiplicative-constant counterexamples
isolate the roles of invariance and calibration. Biseparability and zero
annihilation are consequences of the calibrated sufficient set
$\{\textup{(A0)},\textup{AX4},\textup{AX6b},\textup{AX8}\}$, not independent
commitments. The load-bearing axioms themselves are normative commitments
of the framework, defended in Section~\ref{sec:objections} rather than
derived.
\item \textbf{A suite of theorems with falsification windows}
(Section~\ref{sec:theorems}): coverage and multi-channel necessity (T2---no
single channel detects all intervention classes; within this instrument
family the decomposition is an attribution cover, with necessity as a
theorem); manipulation strictly reduces autonomy only under explicit
relative-baseline strictness premises and a positive baseline, while covert
manipulation can inflate a measurable hallucination index (T3);
highest-leverage attack concentration and water-filling defense under
log-linear damage (T4);
loss of autonomy as a six-way disjunction (T5); an explicit autonomy margin
$\deltastar=\min(\theta/W,\kappa,\lceil(r{+}1)/2\rceil c_0/W)$ with a
positive retention floor
$\Amin$---including an iid Gaussian mean-shift lower bound showing that
expected drift damage at detection diverges as $\alpha\downarrow0$ at fixed
nonzero drift, with no universal fixed-$\alpha$ weak-drift claim---and a
certificate-failure band under the registered upper comparison model, with
capture reported separately for ATK-6's exact saturating implementation (T6);
component-level delegation laws with a two-error bracket (T7); and
coercion consistency for direct goal implantation and action compression
(T8).
\item \textbf{Identifiability theory} (Section~\ref{sec:identifiability}):
white-box point identification, tiered by the modal commitments each channel
carries; a certified \emph{one-sided} black-box bound for $\Aorg$ under
bypass-semantics probes (the reference lower end is uncertified); a valid
one-sided direction for $\Aexec$ only under independent probes, with passive
direction otherwise unidentified; and Proposition~\ref{prop:unidentifiable}
(implanted endorsement is behaviorally indistinguishable from endogenous
endorsement), which proves the need for non-behavioral provenance or
mechanism evidence while leaving full causal logs as one sufficient route,
not a uniquely necessary architecture.
\item \textbf{Illustrative simulations with registered evidence boundaries}
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}): a 4{,}620-run campaign over fifteen
scenarios---six attack classes, delegation, helplessness induction, resource
starvation, capability-envelope erosion, forced autonomy, black-box
degradation, cross-framework consistency, and an execution dynamic-range
arm, plus an adaptive-adversary supplement. The campaign is an
implementation-level consistency check, and we report it as such, separating
mathematical status, criterion outcomes, evidence type, and external
validation rather than issuing a single ``confirmed theories'' tally. T7 is
inconclusive because the corrected two-error bracket is non-diagnostic on the
registered DEL-1 arms. The original E2-X1 criterion failed before its post hoc
replacement, and the X5-a absolute-reading falsifier fired. New R14
counterexample and optimizer tests are separate analytic checks. The newly
executed time-stratified X5-c implements $\Lambda$ on an explicit SCM under
Gaussian and logistic noise; it reports model-sensitive two-model bands and
does not upgrade legacy marker scenarios. A separate C0-CF construction uses
the same environment and full passive $(E,O,A)$ laws. Adaptive adversaries supply a separate sensitivity calibration of
the black-box origination bound's premise (BB-2). Verdicts are
stable under alternative aggregation weights (Kendall $\tau\ge0.93$) and
under axiom-relaxation robustness checks in the elasticity parameter; on the
default instance the execution channel's narrow band makes that statement an
effectively four-channel one, so the same weight battery was rerun on the
rich-coupling EXEC-1 instance (fifth channel at full dynamic range): no
verdict flips there either.
\item \textbf{An interpretive correspondence with existing frameworks}
(Proposition~C0,
\S\ref{sec:theorems} and \S\ref{sec:discussion}): under explicit definitional
readings---whose faithfulness to the source texts is registered as
hermeneutic, not mathematical, residual uncertainty---the agency profiles of
\citet{dung2024}, the four dimensions of \citet{kasirzadeh2025}, and the
user roles of \citet{feng2025} admit only restricted explanatory comparisons,
not general rank consistency. A same-environment constructive witness shows
that identical full-trajectory $(E^T,O^T,A^T)$ laws can coexist with different
epistemic accuracy and counterfactual endorsement deviation
(Proposition~C0(ii)). It does not assert identical full external profiles or
construct validation. These mappings are explanatory correspondences, not
mathematical special cases, subsumption, or a unification theorem.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Map of the paper.} Section~\ref{sec:related-work} positions the
framework in seven literatures and states the gaps it fills.
Section~\ref{sec:framework} gives the operational definition, the system
model, and the intervention taxonomy. Section~\ref{sec:axioms} states the axioms and
the six channel measures. Section~\ref{sec:representation} proves the
representation theorem. Section~\ref{sec:theorems} presents the theorem suite
with proof sketches and falsification windows; full proofs are in the
appendices. Section~\ref{sec:identifiability} develops the identifiability
analysis. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} answers the standing debates and
registers limitations; Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes. Appendix~
\ref{sec:experiments} presents simulation and implementation evidence,
followed by the technical proofs.
